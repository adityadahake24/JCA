About

From Manhattan living rooms to a temple of five traditions

Fifty years ago, JCA was a handful of families meeting informally. Today it's the first Jain center registered in America, and still growing.

1965–2026

Fifty years of history

1965
The first Jain families arrive in New York; informal gatherings begin in Manhattan homes and halls.
1976
JCA is organized and registered — the first Jain Center in America.
1981
The first temple building is purchased, in Queens.
June 2005
The Elmhurst facility is inaugurated — later named Best Religious Building of the year in Queens.
Ongoing
The Asthapad Maha Tirth — 24 Tirthankara idols in crystal and gemstone — takes shape on the fourth floor.
Mission

One roof, every tradition, equally honored

JCA exists to preserve and share Jain religious and cultural heritage for its community — and to do it in a way few Jain centers attempt, by uniting Shvetambar Murtipujak, Sthanakvasi, Digambar, and Shrimad Rajchandra practice under a single roof, with equal reverence for each.

Fifty years ago, a handful of families gathered in a Manhattan apartment. Today, five traditions worship under one roof — and every member is part of what comes next.
